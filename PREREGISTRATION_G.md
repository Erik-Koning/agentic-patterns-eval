## 1. Questions

Study G asks whether context management and multi-agent topology still pay as the agent model gets more capable: the "bitter lesson" question of CONTEXT_MANAGEMENT_AUDIT §1–2. It asks it on long F8 "shift" sessions that overflow a fixed context window. HYPOTHESES.md G1–G3 become three questions:

1. **Headroom (G2).** At every capability point, is there a gap between a single agent that keeps its full history (CM0) and one shown the true shift state (O-state)? How much of that gap does each context-management strategy recover, does that change with capability, and do the gains grow with session length?
2. **Decomposition (G3).** Does a specialised team (M2) beat a single agent (S1) in long sessions? Does isolation (M1: identical workers in fresh contexts) carry at least half of that gain? Does a single agent with the best context-management stack (S-CM\*) recover most of it at a fraction of the team's cost per solved item?
3. **Convergence (G1).** Does the team's advantage over the managed single agent shrink as measured capability rises?

- **Confirmatory:** G-H2a, and the fixed sequence G-H3-pre → G-H3a → G-H3b.
- **Descriptive** (D-033: no affordable design gives them useful power): G-H1, G-H2b, and the rows for strategies, length, probes, failures and mixed models.
- **Capability axis:** four points, GPT-6 Luna at low and high reasoning effort, Sol high and Astra high (§5).
- **Platform:** the custom Inspect harness (`src/ape`, Inspect AI 0.3.273) on OpenAI (D-000).

## 2. Hypotheses, estimands and tests

### 2.1 Conventions (D-029, D-032, D-038, D-047)

- **Sessions are the clusters.** A session is one F8 world (§4.3). Its epochs are pooled first, since they share the world. A world run at several capability points is one cluster across them (cells of one length and knob variant read the same seeds, §4.1).
- **Outcome.** A session's item success rate, pooled over epochs. An item fails when it is answered wrongly, never reached, lost to the overflow rule (§3.2), left unscored by an errored sample (as in the gate), or in progress when a sample limit ended the session (D-047; the report then fails too). Binary session success (every item and the report exact) is reported with pass^k and never tested: over 20–40 items it is near 0 for real agents.
- **Decision test: the session-clustered t.** Every confirmatory estimand is a linear combination L = Σ_c a_c ḡ_c of per-point session means of a per-session value (a paired difference, a linear form of several arms, or a linearised ratio).
  - The variance of L is unbiased with each shared world one cluster across points; with no shared world it is Welch's.
  - The df are Welch–Satterthwaite, capped at G_eff − 1 when worlds are shared, where G_eff is the model-based effective number of clusters (D-038). G_eff is about 12.6 of 16 in the topology design, and G in a balanced design.
  - **The decision level is calibrated: each confirmatory t-test rejects when its one-sided p is at most 0.013** (`g_hypotheses.T_LEVEL`; D-047, review finding S-4). At a nominal 0.025 the t rejected true nulls at up to 0.037 in G-H3's proportion tests (arm-specific session variance), 0.040 in its cost clause (heavy-tailed costs) and 0.037 in G-H2a at Astra (4 sessions, a wider O-state session spread): the variance is unbiased, but the statistic's tails are heavier than t_df's. No small-sample df correction (Bell–McCaffrey, G_eff − 2, kurtosis-aware) or skewness correction brought every row to 0.025. At 0.013 the worst of 72 stress scenarios rejects at 0.024 in G-H3 and 0.021–0.023 in G-H2a (16,000 and 20,000 simulated studies per scenario, and the review's own runs), at the planned sizes and at the minimum sessions below. The confirmatory rows' intervals are the matching (1 − 2 × 0.013) = 97.4% intervals; every other interval is 95%.
  - **Minimum sessions per planned point** (`g_hypotheses.min_sessions`): max(3, ⌈0.6 × planned⌉) usable sessions, never more than planned. Topology: Luna-low and Luna-high 10 of 16, Sol-high 5 of 8. Context management: Luna 6 of 10, Sol 4 of 6, Astra 3 of 4. A planned point below its minimum is missing, and its family INCOMPLETE (§9.1). These are the smallest sizes the simulation validated at 0.013: with every topology point at its minimum the worst row rejects at 0.023, while Sol at 4 sessions reaches 0.027.
  - It is the gate's validated world-clustered t (D-023), generalised to any weights. It is primary, rather than a sign-flip, because Astra's 4 sessions give a sign-flip floor of 1/16 = 0.0625 (D-032).
- **Reported beside it, never decisive:** the wild session-level sign-flip with null-restricted residuals (exact up to 16 clusters), with its minimum attainable p; and the session bootstrap (percentile), which the gate found anti-conservative with few clusters.
- **α.** One-sided **0.025** per confirmatory family (G-H2-gap and G-H3), with no correction across the two families (as in the main study, D-031); each test is decided at the calibrated 0.013 that holds it. Descriptive intervals are two-sided 95%.
- **Ratios** (the isolation share, the recovery, R_x) are tested as linear forms, (N − r D) > 0, which needs no ratio inference. The ratios themselves get delta-method, Fieller and bootstrap intervals, and are not estimated when their denominator is ≤ 2 pp.
- **Scale.** Gaps, shares, headroom and cost ratios are on the probability scale. G-H1's slope is on the empirical-logit scale, log((k + 0.5) / (n − k + 0.5)): near the ceiling a constant logit effect shrinks in pp (audit §2.1).
- **Cost per solved item:** Σ cost / Σ items solved per arm and point. Cache-adjusted $ is the primary meter, because compaction and pruning break cached prefixes (audit §5.1); tokens, calls and wall-clock are reported. Management (`cm`) calls are included and probe calls excluded.
- **Implementation:** `ape.analysis.g_stats` (`gap_iut`, `gh3`, `gh1`, `tost`, `headroom`, …) and `g_report.decisions`, called by `ape.analyze_g.analyze` at the frozen commit.

### 2.3 Confirmatory hypotheses

**G-H2a: headroom at every capability point** (family G-H2-gap).
- **Estimand:** per point, Gap_T = s(O-state) − s(CM0): the mean over sessions of the paired difference in item success rate. It uses the long sessions: N = 40 at Luna-low, Luna-high and Sol-high, and N = 24 at Astra-high.
- **Test:** intersection-union. Each point gets a one-sided t at the calibrated 0.013 (§2.1), with no adjustment (an intersection-union test needs none). G-H2a is SUPPORTED only if every point rejects.
- **Astra:** its 4 sessions cannot reach α by sign-flip, so the decision rests on the t there. Simulated type I at 0.013: 0.012–0.013 per point under g_power's model (20,000 studies), and at most 0.023 under the review's stress scenarios (Astra with a wider O-state session spread, which reached 0.037 at a nominal 0.025).
- **Expected:** CM0 fails every item after its overflow, which falls at a median 0.70 of the session (D-028). So the gap is about 25–30 pp by construction: power 0.94 at the calibrated level, limited by Astra's 4 sessions (0.99 at a nominal 0.025; 0.996 with 6 Astra sessions). G-H2a establishes that the headroom exists at every point; how much of it each strategy recovers is G-H2c's question (descriptive).

**G-H3: decomposition, a fixed sequence at 0.025** (family G-H3; audit §2.3, §7).
- **Points:** pooled with equal weights over Luna-low, Luna-high and Sol-high, where S1, M1, M2 and S-CM\* all run. Astra runs S1 and M2 only.
- **Sequence:** a step is tested only if every earlier one rejects; otherwise it is NOT_TESTED.

1. **G-H3-pre** (the gatekeeper): M2 − S1 > 0. Without a gain the shares are undefined. Power 1.0 at the expected +28–33 pp.
2. **G-H3a** (isolation carries at least half): (M1 − S1) − 0.5 (M2 − S1) > 0.
   - The isolation share (M1 − S1) / (M2 − S1) is reported with Fieller and bootstrap intervals. The specialisation share is its complement (G-H3-spec).
   - Power 0.99 at a share of 0.75, 0.83 at 0.70 and 0.55 at 0.65, at the calibrated 0.013 (0.997, 0.90 and 0.66 at a nominal 0.025).
3. **G-H3b** (a managed single agent recovers it more cheaply): an intersection-union of two one-sided t-tests at the calibrated 0.013, both required.
   - Recovery: (S-CM\* − S1) − 0.8 (M2 − S1) > 0.
   - Cost: log(CPS(S-CM\*) / CPS(M2)) < log 0.6, where CPS is the cost per solved item in cache-adjusted $.
   - Power 0.56 at a recovery of 0.95 and a cost ratio of 0.45, and 0.26 at 0.90 and 0.50, at the calibrated 0.013 (0.69 and 0.37 at a nominal 0.025, which over-rejected: §2.1). The cost clause alone has power ≥ 0.96 at a ratio ≤ 0.5.
   - A cost that cannot be computed (a session's meter missing) makes G-H3b NOT_TESTABLE, never NOT_SUPPORTED.
   - G-H3b is the study's weakest confirmatory test, so a NOT_SUPPORTED there is weak evidence against it.

**What G-H3 measures: isolation against a harness-enforced window (D-040).**
- **The window.** W = 32,000 tokens is enforced by the harness, identically at every tier, whatever each model's native window (§3.2).
- **The team does not overflow.** M1's and M2's orchestrator holds the cases, its delegations and answers, and the workers' results, each clipped at 2,000 tokens. The bulky customer and order files stay in the workers' fresh contexts. As D-040 measured, at N = 20 the orchestrator never overflows.
- **S1 does.** S1 is CM0: its full history crosses W at a median 0.70 of the session on the reference trajectory, and earlier with a real agent's text and reasoning (D-028). From then on the harness fails every remaining item and the report.
- **So M1 − S1 is largely S1 overflowing while the team does not.** That is the isolation mechanism G-H3 is about, but measured **against a harness-enforced window**. M2 − S1, the isolation share and S-CM\*'s recovery all carry S1's overflow rule.
- **The claim.** A supported G-H3a reads: under a hard 32K window, keeping bulky tool output in isolated worker contexts carries at least half of the team's gain. It does not show that isolation pays for a single agent whose native window holds the whole session.
- **How much of each gap is the window.** The report gives S1's overflow position and rate per point, and G-H1's S1-pre comparison: M2 against S1 on the items before S1's overflow.

### 2.4 Descriptive rows

No decision rests on these. Each is an estimate with its 95% interval (session-clustered t, with the bootstrap beside it). An interval that excludes 0 is reported as such, never as a confirmed effect.

- **G-H1 (convergence; descriptive since D-033).**
  - **Reference: S-CM\*.** The estimand is the slope of the per-session gap logit(M2) − logit(S-CM\*) on measured capability (§5.2; OLS over sessions), and the change along the fitted line over the capability span. It covers Luna-low, Luna-high and Sol-high.
  - Neither S-CM\* nor any topology arm runs at Astra: the Astra topology cell `g.topo.astra` (S1, M2) is off by default (D-051; `enabled: false`, switchable back on), so G-H1 and its S1-pre sensitivity span Luna-low, Luna-high and Sol.
  - **Sensitivity: S1-pre,** M2 − S1 on the items before S1's overflow in each session, at all four points. It does not drift under a constant mechanism.
  - **S1 as scored: descriptive only.** Its gap carries CM0's overflow rule, so it drifts by +0.67 logit over the span even when nothing changes; S-CM\*'s drifts by +0.24.
  - **G-H1-M1:** the same with M1, at the three points where M1 runs.
  - **Precision:** the change over the span has an SD of 0.29 logit. The interval excludes 0 in 26% of studies when M2's advantage falls from 0.9 logit to 0 across the points (S1-pre: 39%). A test would have power 0.12, and no affordable design reaches 0.8 (D-033).
- **G-H2b (persistence across capability; descriptive since D-033).**
  - The estimand is θ_x, the slope of R_x,c = (s_x − s_CM0) / (s_O − s_CM0) on measured capability times the span. It is computed for CM-sum and CM-todo, the strategies run at every point.
  - R_x is scale-free and not driven by CM0's overflow rule.
  - The TOST at ±0.20 R is still computed but not decided (its power was 0.01).
  - Sensitivity: the gain s_x − s_CM0 on the logit scale. It drifts +0.16 to +0.22 over the span under a constant R_x.
  - Precision: θ has an SD of 0.14 R.
- **G-H2c (headroom recovered).** R_x,c per strategy and point, with delta-method, Fieller and bootstrap intervals; undefined when the headroom is ≤ 2 pp. CM-sum and CM-todo run at every point, CM-prune and CM-reset at Luna-high, CM-native at Luna-high and Sol-high (§3.5, D-043).
- **G-H2d (length).**
  - Per arm × point: logistic slopes of item success on log(view tokens at the decision call), on relative position, and on both with their interaction. The intervals are session-clustered (sandwich), and items lost to overflow are left out (audit §5.2).
  - The gain over CM0 before and after the reference W crossing.
  - The short-session control: Gap(N = 40) − Gap(N = 10) at Luna-high (Welch over sessions; §4.4).
  - CM0 fails every item after its overflow, so "gains grow with length" holds for CM0 by construction. The managed arms' and O-state's slopes carry the information. CM0's own slope per point is the audit's "absorption into the model" signal.
- **G-H3-spec.** The specialisation share, (M2 − M1) / (M2 − S1).
- **G-D1 (forked state probes).** Mean probe F1 per checkpoint, arm and point (a missed checkpoint scores 0), with session-clustered intervals. Also the probe-to-behaviour correlation: probe F1 at k against the success of the dependency items after k (audit §5.3). A weak correlation means the probes measure something the policy does not use.
- **G-D2 (failure taxonomy).** Counts and rates per item, on failed items (audit §5.4): forgot constraint, stale state, resurrected done item, dropped item, hallucinated state and overflow.
- **G-D3 (mixed models; D-029).** success ~ topology × capability and success ~ strategy × capability, with session and item random intercepts (statsmodels `BinomialBayesMixedGLM`, variational Bayes). No claim rests on them.

**Not tested here:**
- the audit's equivalence form of G-H2 (it is now G-H2b's estimate);
- a fixed-summariser sensitivity run (audit §6.3): the `cm` role is the agent's own model;
- CM-trim (built, not planned);
- the Tier B arms (§3.5).

### 2.5 Power priors

The power and precision in §2.2–2.4 assume:
- σ_world 0.5, σ_item 1.0, σ_arm 0.3, σ_epoch 0.2, σ_mix 0.5;
- a W crossing at 0.70 of the session, ± 0.06 by world and ± 0.03 by epoch;
- base success 0.65 / 0.75 / 0.82 / 0.88 and measured capability 0.72 / 0.80 / 0.86 / 0.90 at Luna-low / Luna-high / Sol-high / Astra-high.

They stay assumptions until the micro-pilot (§4.5). G-H3a and G-H3b lose power if σ_arm is larger: at σ_arm 0.6, G-H3a's power is 0.90 at a share of 0.80 and 0.58 at 0.70, and G-H3b's 0.35 at a recovery of 0.95 (at the calibrated 0.013).

## 3. Arms

### 3.1 One loop for every arm

Every arm runs the F8 session loop (`agent/session.py`) on the ContextPolicy layer (`agent/context_policy.py`, D-030).
- **Shared:** the system prompt (the base prompt, the whole corpus, the shift rules), the session tools, the per-case turn cap (8 turns, one nudge), the report phase (one nudge) and the probes.
- **Only the view differs.** The loop keeps the full, append-only history; it is what the log shows and where the scorer's tool events come from. At every call the loop asks the arm's policy for the **view**, the messages the model sees.
- **What a policy may add:** its own tools and a short, fixed system-prompt addendum, nothing else.
- **Every call is recorded** with its kind (`agent`, `cm` for management, `probe`), view tokens, model and usage, so management and probe cost separate from agent cost.
- **Resume.** A session checkpoints after every item, and a retried sample resumes after its last completed item (D-030; §8).

### 3.2 Window W, threshold T_abs and the overflow rule

| | Value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| W, the nominal window | **32,000 tokens** (`config/run_plan.yaml` `study_g.window`) | Enforced by the harness, identical for every arm and tier, whatever the model's native window (D-021). |
| T_abs, the threshold | **20,000 tokens** (`study_g.threshold`) | Absolute, identical for every arm and tier. Fixed, not tuned: the runner never passes another. Level-triggered: the policy's `on_threshold` hook runs before every call whose view exceeds it. Inspect's strategies are applied from this hook, never at Inspect's own threshold, which counts tokens differently. |
| Meter | o200k tokens over message text and tool calls | Tool schemas are not counted, a policy's own included (`todo_write`'s is about 800 tokens; recorded as `policy_tool_tokens`). CM-native's opaque block counts as everything it replaced until the next agent call reports the provider's input; after that, as that input minus the meter's count of the rest of the view, which over-counts it (conservative). |

- **Overflow.** An agent call whose view, or a management call whose input, would exceed W is an overflow. That item and every later one fail, and so does the report.
  - A management overflow at an item boundary fails from the next item; the item that just ended still counts.
  - In M1 and M2, W applies to every agent's input, the orchestrator's and the workers'. An overflow anywhere ends the session.
  - Probes are not W-checked.
- This rule is what makes CM0 and S1 fail after the crossing. It is the harness-enforced window of §2.3.

### 3.3 Forked state probes (never appended)

- **When:** after the items k ∈ {5, 15, 30, 45, 60} with k ≤ N.
  - N = 40: after 5, 15 and 30. N = 24 and N = 20: after 5 and 15. N = 10: after 5.
  - At each item boundary the order is: the policy's `after_item`, then the probe (it sees the post-boundary view), then the checkpoint.
- **How:** the probe question goes to the policy's **probe view**, what the model would see next (for M1 and M2, the orchestrator's). It is a side call with role `probe`, on the agent's own model and effort. The strict JSON schema has five lists: completed, pending, memos in force, open follow-ups and escalated.
- **Never appended.** The answer never enters the history, and the probe view must leave the policy's state unchanged: the loop checks `state_dict()` before and after.
- **Scored** as F1 per category against the generator's `state_at(k)` (G-D1); a missed checkpoint scores 0. Probe calls are excluded from every cost meter and reported apart.

### 3.4 The arms

**View layout of every managed arm** (D-037):
1. the system prompt;
2. the start message (the task spec);
3. the notes, if any (a summary or a handoff note);
4. the completed cases still shown (after pruning, a compacted window of them);
5. the todo list, if any;
6. the current case.

The current case is never compacted, summarised or dropped. When a view exceeds T_abs, the compactor (prune) acts first, with no model call. If the view is still over, the mover (sum, reset or todo) drops the completed messages.

### 4.3 F8 sessions and the window crossing

- **One session is one F8 world** (`worlds/gen_f8.py`): a queue of N cases handled in order by one agent, then an end-of-shift report. The cases are F7-10 policy decisions and F3-5 tickets from worlds of the same seed, merged.
- **What creates context demand:**
  - bulky customer and order files, about `output_tokens` each (0.6–1.4×);
  - mid-session memos, which override the handbook from their case until they are withdrawn;
  - cross-item dependencies: a quota of 2 approvals per customer per shift, follow-ups that name only the earlier case ID, and deferred escalations;
  - the report.
- **Order.** Item kinds are shuffled within a session, so position is not confounded with case type.
- **The crossing (D-028).** The knobs make every long cell cross W at a median 0.70 of the session on the reference trajectory (20 reference sessions per setting):
  - 40-case cells at the default 1,000-token files: 0.70;
  - 20-case cells at 2,250 tokens: 0.70;
  - the 24-case Astra cell at 1,750 tokens: 0.71.
  - Real agents add text and reasoning to the view, so they cross earlier. A test checks the plan's crossings.
- **Item outcomes** (programmatic, `scorers/session.py`):
  - A policy case or follow-up succeeds when the first decision submitted in its own window equals the gold.
  - A ticket succeeds when its order's mutating calls equal the gold calls and it was closed with `finish`.
  - Dependency items are those whose correctness needs earlier state.

### 4.4 The short-session control

`g.cm.luna-n10` runs CM0 and O-state at Luna-high on 10-case sessions at the default knobs, which never cross W. It separates session-level effects (the report's burden, the shift rules) from the window, through Gap(N = 40) − Gap(N = 10) at Luna-high (G-H2d).

### 4.5 The micro-pilot and the sizes

- **The sizes are D-033's.** No rule re-sizes them from pilot data.
- **What the micro-pilot measures.** It runs the 7 Luna CM arms on 3 pilot sessions each (N = 40, 1 epoch): calls per item, view sizes, cache shares, management overheads and crossing positions.
  - `ape.budget calibrate` replaces the cost priors from its logs automatically (the run's `config/budget_calibration_measured.yaml`), and every later phase's budget check uses them.
- **The topology pilot (planned, D-047).** A short `g.pilot.topo` cell runs the four topology arms, S1, M1, M2 and S-CM\*, on 2 pilot sessions each at N = 20, so the team arms are exercised before the freeze as the CM arms are. The run plan gains the cell before the freeze; this section describes it as planned.
- **Session failure rates (planned, D-047).** For every micro-pilot cell and arm, the micro-pilot summary reports the share of sessions that errored, that hit a sample limit (tokens, messages, time or working time) and that overflowed W. A limit fails the item in progress and the report (§9.1).
- **Before the freeze, the analyst reviews:**
  - CM0 overflows in the long sessions (near 0.70) and O-state does not;
  - CM-native compacts (its support record) and no arm fails at start;
  - the team arms complete their `g.pilot.topo` sessions, and the session error, limit and overflow rates per cell and arm;
  - calls per item and views against the priors (§7);

- **The point** is `<tier>-<effort>`, from each cell's profile and effort (`g_load`).
- **Self-summarisation.** No profile sets a `cm` role, so management calls use the agent's own model and effort. No profile sets a `probe` role either, so the probes ask the model that is in the session.
- **The axis is measured capability, not the tier label** (audit §2.1). Tier labels confound capability with context window, price, speed and default effort. Four points support a trend with an interval, not proof of future convergence.
- **Pinned models.** The model IDs are aliases. The readiness probe pins each served snapshot in `PROVENANCE.md`, and live preflight refuses a run whose probe sees a different snapshot.

### 5.2 The capability anchor

- **Measure.** S1, the agent arm with the whole corpus in its system prompt, on F7-10 and F3-5: short-horizon held-out tasks, 48 per cell (4 test worlds × 12 tasks), 1 epoch, at every point. Capability is the equal-weight mean of the two cells' task means, with a world-clustered standard error (`g_load.capability_table`).
- **Use.** It enters only G-H1's and G-H2b's slopes (descriptive) and the mixed models. The confirmatory rows do not use it.
- **Near the ceiling.**
  - The priors put measured capability at about 0.72 / 0.80 / 0.86 / 0.90, with a standard error near 0.035 per point, so Sol-high and Astra-high may not separate.
  - The report flags adjacent points whose difference is within 2 standard errors.
  - Options:
    - (a) keep F7-10 and F3-5, as planned;
    - (b) a harder anchor (F7-1000 and F3-60), in new plan cells;
    - (c) keep it, and add the tier order (the points as ranks) as a sensitivity axis for G-H1 and G-H2b.
  - Costs are estimated before the decision.
  - **Decision (D-043): (c).** The anchor stays F7-10 and F3-5, and the tier order (Luna-low 1, Luna-high 2, Sol-high 3, Astra-high 4) is a pre-registered sensitivity axis for G-H1 and G-H2b, reported beside each measured-capability estimate (`g_hypotheses.TIER_ORDER`). A harder anchor (F7-100 and F3-60, +$45) was not chosen.

### 5.3 API mode

- **One API mode per point.** Every arm at a capability point runs on the same API mode: Inspect's own choice for the agent model, which is the Responses API for GPT-6. Management calls, workers and probes use the same model and path.
- **Recorded.** The readiness probe (`readiness/probe_openai.py`, extended in B11 to Sol, Astra and native compaction) records per model the API Inspect uses, the efforts it honours and whether native compaction is supported. CM-native runs only where it is.
- **Reported.** The report states, per tier, which model and snapshot ran on which API (audit §9.2).

## 6. Tuning (dev split only; equal budget)

- **Grid:** `config/tuning_grid_study_g.yaml`, run by `python -m ape.run_study tune --study study_g --run-id <id>` on F8-40 dev sessions at Luna-high (`g.tune.luna`; 3 sessions × 1 epoch per candidate). Its systems are the tuned arms: CM-prune, CM-sum, CM-todo, CM-reset and S-CM\* (BUILD_PLAN B11).
- **Equal budget.** Every system gets at most `budget_per_system` candidates (3 in the plan). Its owner declares them in advance, before any dev run, and they are evaluated on the same dev sessions. The runner refuses a system with more.
- **Candidates** are `{id, arm, env}`: an arm and its `APE_CM_*` knobs. W, T_abs, the loop, the prompts and the turn cap cannot be tuned.
- **Selection** (pre-registered, `ape.tuning.select`): the highest mean dev item success. Candidates within `tie_pp` (1 pp) of the best go to the cheaper one, by Inspect's cost.
- **Logged.** Every candidate tried goes to `runs/study_g/<id>/tune/tuning_log.jsonl`. A re-run archives the previous log beside it, and archived logs count.
  - A PC6-style completeness check is reported: a record per declared candidate, no more than the budget, and each selection logged. It warns and does not block the freeze (D-036).

  - Every tuned arm runs at test with exactly the knobs it was selected with, and no arm runs with a knob that another arm's selection set.

  - CM0 and O-state (no knobs);

## 8. Validity controls

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| Window confound (fractional thresholds, native windows) | Absolute T_abs and a harness-enforced W, identical across arms and tiers (§3.2). The price: G-H3 measures isolation against that window (§2.3), and nothing here tests management beyond 32K tokens of absolute context. |
| Provider-feature confound | One API mode per point (§5.3). CM-native runs only where the probe confirms support. Models and snapshots are recorded per tier. |
| A CM strategy inside the topology contrast | The team's orchestrator follows the CM0 rule (D-040), and S1 is CM0. |
| Cache economics | Cache-adjusted $ is the primary cost meter; tokens, calls and wall-clock are reported. |
| Summariser capability | It is part of the treatment: self-summarisation at each point's model and effort. A fixed-summariser sensitivity run is not in the plan. |
| Position vs difficulty | Item kinds are shuffled within a session; the mixed models carry item random effects. |
| Probe validity and leakage | Probes run on a side branch: never appended, policy state unchanged (checked), excluded from cost. Probe F1 is not an outcome, and the probe-to-behaviour correlation is reported. |
| Grader leakage | Management calls see only the session transcript, rendered as plain text, never scores or gold. This study has no lesson writing or consolidation. |
| Arm-specific prompting | One base prompt, tool set and turn cap. Each arm adds only its tools and a short, fixed addendum. The management prompts ask every mechanism for the same content (`cm_prompts.WHAT_TO_KEEP`). The M1 and M2 prompts differ only where specialisation does. An independent prompt author reviews them (§6). |
| Tuning asymmetry | The tuned CM arms get equal candidate budgets. S-CM\* is tuned (the single agent's best foot forward) while M1 and M2 run at their built defaults. That favours S-CM\* in G-H3b; it is stated, not corrected. |
| Knob leakage between arms | The CM knobs share one namespace (D-037). Each tuned arm runs at test with exactly its own selection's knobs (§6), and the task metadata records every `APE_CM_*` value a session ran with. |
| Resumed sessions | A retried session resumes after its last completed item. The interrupted item reruns from its first call, so a resumed session is not bit-identical to an uninterrupted one. Every resume is recorded (`f8_resume`) and reported. |
| Errored samples | An errored sample's unscored items fail, so an arm that errors more pays for it. Error counts are reported per arm and point. |
| Test-split leakage | The test split is generated after the freeze, from the run's own seed block, under Study G's own lock. Dev and pilot worlds never overlap it. |
| Model churn | Snapshots are pinned (PROVENANCE.md). A re-run on a new model release is a new study run: the living-benchmark design (audit §2.1). |
| Synthetic realism | F8 is synthetic and structured. This round has no public long-horizon anchor (audit §9.9). |

## 9. Decision and reporting rules

### 9.1 Decisions

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | The confirmatory row's t-test rejects at its calibrated one-sided level (§2.1; the family's α is 0.025) with every planned point present: for G-H2a at every planned point, for G-H3b both clauses. |
| INCOMPLETE | A planned point lacks paired data or has fewer usable sessions than its minimum (§2.1), or the run plan cannot be read (so the planned points are unknown); the missing points are named, and the estimate and decision over the points present are reported beside it. Never counted as SUPPORTED. |
| NOT_SUPPORTED | Tested, and it does not reject. |
| NOT_TESTED | An earlier step of G-H3's sequence was not supported. |
| NOT_TESTABLE | Arms, points or sessions are missing (fewer than 2 paired sessions at a point), or a clause cannot be computed (G-H3b's cost when a session's meter is missing); the reason is given. Never NOT_SUPPORTED. |
| descriptive | G-H1, G-H1-M1, G-H2b, G-H2c, G-H2d, G-H3-spec and G-D1–G-D3: an estimate with its interval, never a decision. |

- **Missing data.** A cell that fails or that the budget stops is reported in the coverage table with its reason. No value is imputed.
  - G-H3 pools its planned points (Luna-low, Luna-high, Sol-high) and G-H2a tests its planned points (all four). If any planned point lacks paired data, or has fewer usable sessions than its minimum (§2.1), the row is labelled **INCOMPLETE**, never SUPPORTED, and the report names the missing points; the estimate over the points present (a point below its minimum is left out), and the decision those points alone would give, are reported beside it (`g_stats.gap_iut`, `g_stats.gh3`, `g_report.planned_points_and_sessions`). A missing planned point is a deviation.
  - The planned points and sessions come from the run's frozen `config/run_plan.yaml`. If it cannot be read, a live analysis stops with an error; an offline one labels G-H2a and G-H3 INCOMPLETE.
- **Errored samples** count as failures (§2.1). **Sample limits** (tokens, messages, time, working time): the item in progress when the limit fired fails, even if an answer was recorded in the turn that hit it; items completed before it stand; a limit in the report phase fails the report (D-047).
- **Section failures.** Each report section is computed in isolation: one that fails is listed under the report's section errors, and the other sections and the decisions stand.
- **Re-runs.** A failed group is re-run by resuming it. Nothing is re-run because of its result.
- **No extension.** Study G pre-registers no extension and spends no α on one. A later run, including one frozen with `--extension-of`, is analysed alone, reported as a replication, and never pooled with this run: `ape.analyze_g` labels such a run's report "REPLICATION of `<primary run>`".

### 9.2 Reading guide (audit §2.4)

This is how the results will be read; it is not a decision rule.

| Result | Reading |
|---|---|
| G-H2a supported, and R_x stable across capability (G-H2b's estimate near 0) | Context management keeps its value as models improve: invest in it rather than in new topologies. |
| G-H2a supported, and CM0's own degradation slope flattens with capability | The model absorbs part of the problem (the bitter lesson through the model); the remaining value is cost and latency. |
| CM-native ≈ the best harness strategy where it runs | Context management is absorbed into the provider, not made unnecessary. Assessed at the two points where CM-native runs (Luna-high, Sol-high; §3.5), descriptively. |
| Gains only after the W crossing (G-H2d) | Context management is capacity engineering (it enables longer work), not an accuracy technique. |
| G-H3a supported | Under a hard window, isolation carries at least half of the team's gain, and specialisation the rest (§2.3). |
| G-H3b supported | A single agent with context management is the cheaper way to get the team's gain in long sessions. |
| G-H1's estimate negative, with an interval excluding 0 | The topology gap shrinks with capability. It is reported as an estimate, not as a confirmed trend. |

### 9.3 Reporting (always, whatever the results)
